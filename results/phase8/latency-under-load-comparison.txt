Phase 8 — Latency Under Load Comparison
========================================

All tests: UE (10.45.0.2) → probe/load destinations, 300-packet ping,
200 ms interval, ~60 s duration, 8 Mbps UDP load when applicable.

| Condition                | Probe dst | Load dst | Ping avg | Ping mdev | Loss |
|--------------------------|-----------|----------|----------|-----------|------|
| Control (idle)           | central   | none     | 77.4 ms  | 21.1 ms   | 0%   |
| Same-UPF under load      | central   | central  | 65.4 ms  | 13.1 ms   | 0%   |
| Cross-UPF (edge probe)   | edge      | central  | 29.7 ms  | 10.8 ms   | 0%   |
| Cross-UPF (central probe)| central   | edge     | 31.7 ms  | 11.6 ms   | 0%   |

Decomposition of Effects
------------------------
Effect 1 — Scheduler warm-up (idle → same-UPF load):
  77.4 → 65.4 ms = -12.0 ms (-15.5%)

Effect 2 — UPF isolation (same-UPF load → cross-UPF load):
  65.4 → 29.7 ms = -35.7 ms (-54.6%)

Combined effect (idle → cross-UPF load):
  77.4 → 30.7 ms (avg of 29.7 and 31.7) = -46.7 ms (-60.3%)

Symmetric check:
  Edge probe (central loaded): 29.7 ms
  Central probe (edge loaded): 31.7 ms
  Difference: 2.0 ms (below the 10.8 ms jitter floor)

Interpretation
--------------
1. The DU scheduler produces more regular grants when the UE has
   active traffic. Without load, the scheduler occasionally misses
   the ping grant, producing sporadic 100+ ms spikes that raise the
   average. This is a property of the OCUDU implementation, not of
   the 5G standard.

2. When load and probe share the same UPF, they queue against each
   other, adding ~35 ms to the probe.

3. When load and probe are on different UPFs (which are also on
   different DNNs, different TUN interfaces, and different session
   paths), the queueing disappears and the probe returns to the
   warm-scheduler latency of ~30 ms.

Practical conclusion
--------------------
In this software testbed, isolating a latency-sensitive flow onto
a separate UPF (with its own DNN and TUN interface) provides a
measurable ~35 ms latency benefit under load. This is a genuine
data-plane isolation result, distinct from the Phase 7 finding
(which was about simulated backhaul distance).

Limitations
-----------
- Single UE; both flows share the same uplink (that is why the
  scheduler warm-up effect exists at all).
- Software-emulated RAN, not physical RF.
- The specific scheduler behavior is tied to this OCUDU build.
- 8 Mbps UDP is below the UE's ~8.6 Mbps saturation ceiling.
- The conclusion is about average latency under moderate load;
  tail percentiles were not analyzed in this comparison.
