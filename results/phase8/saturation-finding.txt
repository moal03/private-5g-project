Phase 8 — UDP Saturation Finding
================================

Test: UE (10.45.0.2) → Central app (10.60.0.10) via UPF-A
Transport: UDP, 1200-byte payload
Duration: 15-30 s per run

Offered (Mbps) | Delivered (Mbps) | Drain time (s) | Loss
--------------------------------------------------------------
1.0            | 1.00             | —              | 0%
5.0            | 5.00             | —              | 0%
10.0           | 10.00            | —              | 0%
11.0           | 8.55             | 19.30          | 0%
12.0           | control failed   | —              | —
13.0           | 8.60             | 22.68          | 0%
14.0           | control failed   | —              | —
15.0           | control failed   | —              | —
20.0           | control failed   | —              | —
25.0           | control failed   | —              | —
30.0           | control failed   | —              | —

Finding
-------
1. Delivered UDP throughput plateaus at ~8.6 Mbps.
   This is the UE uplink ceiling (matches Phase 4's 8.7 Mbps TCP).
2. Offered loads above 10 Mbps cause the iperf3 TCP control
   channel to be starved by the UDP data flood. Outcome is
   non-deterministic (11 worked, 12 failed, 13 worked, 14 failed).
3. Delivered traffic equal to the ceiling while queue drain
   adds up to ~7 seconds to a 15-second test.
4. Loss is 0% in every successful run — the network itself
   drops no packets. The control channel failure is a tooling
   artifact of iperf3, not a network failure.

Interpretation
--------------
The bottleneck is the UE uplink. Neither the UPF nor the
Open5GS core nor the DU scheduler limits UDP throughput below
that ceiling. Offsetting the UE uplink (e.g., adding more UEs,
using different numerologies) would raise this ceiling, but
those are outside the scope of Phase 8.

Recommendation
--------------
Run subsequent load experiments at 8 Mbps UDP (below the ceiling)
and measure the latency impact on a separate probe flow (ping).
This isolates the question: "How does close-to-saturation load
affect a latency-sensitive probe?"
